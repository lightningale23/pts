        Help

        pts reads Science Bowl questions to you one word at a time, like a moderator. Buzz as soon as you know it, type your answer, and the site checks it.

        1. StartPress N or Tap Start on the Practice page.
        2. BuzzPress SpaceTap the buzzer the moment you know the answer.
        3. AnswerType it and press Enter. It's checked right away.
        4. NextRead the answer, then Ntap Next for another question.

        Practice

        Playing a question

        Each question starts with its subject, type and format ("Physics, Toss-up, Short answer"), then the question appears one word at a time. Buzz whenever you like, even mid-sentence, and you have a few seconds to type your answer.

        • I was right / I was wrong: if the checker got your answer wrong, fix the ruling and your score.
        • Give up shows the answer and waits for you; Skip shows it and moves straight on.
        • Pause stops the reading until you resume.
        • Search Google (after the answer) looks up the whole question, with its choices, in a new tab.

        To change what you get, open Settings beside the question (the menu button at the top on phones): subjects and subcategories, round stages, packets, reading speed, timing and scoring. Show the whole question at once skips the word-by-word reading, and Read the subject and type first turns the lead-in off or on.

        Answering

        Multiple choice

        Type the letter (W, X, Y or Z) or the option's text, as in real matches. This is strict: the text has to match the option (capitals, spaces and punctuation don't matter). A number option can be typed as any equal number in simple form (0.5 for 1/2), but not as 2/4 or an expression. On a touch screen you can tap the letter buttons instead.

        Short answer

        Short answers lean toward accepting. Typos and plurals are fine, and so are lead-ins like "it's" or "the answer is", optional words in parentheses and the answer's listed alternates.

        AnswerAlso accepted
        0.51/2 5 x 10^-1
        9.89.81 (more precise is fine)
        √3⁄2sqrt(3)/2 √3/2 0.866
        ω/41/4 omega w/4 0.25ω
        ½mv²1/2 mv^2
        O horizonO (for "What soil horizon…")
        Doppler effectDoppler (for "Name this effect")

        Math is compared by value, so any equal form works; decimals need at least 3 significant figures. Variables must match, though: M/m isn't m/M. Very short answers like element symbols must be exact, and a near miss that is really another term (troposphere for stratosphere, mitosis for meiosis) isn't forgiven as a typo.

        Lists and order

        For "select all that apply", type the numbers any way you like: 1 and 3, 1, 3, 13 or 31, Roman numerals included (13 works for "I and III"). When the answer is "all", typing every number counts too, and you can answer with the statements' names (alpha, beta, gamma for 1, 2, 3). For "order" or "rank" questions the order matters. On a touch screen, tap the number buttons under the answer box.

        If a ruling is wrong, press I was right (R) and your score is fixed. If an answer key itself is wrong, please Report the question. Once a wrong key is fixed, the packet's old answer shows crossed out under the answer, with the reason it was changed.

        Scoring and timing

        The defaults follow the National Science Bowl. You can change each one under Timing and scoring in the settings.

        Correct toss-up+4
        Correct bonus+10
        Neg (wrong answer, buzzing before the question ends)−4
        Wrong answer after the question ends0
        Time to buzz after a toss-up5 s
        Time to answer after buzzing7 s
        Time for a bonus20 s
        Time for a visual bonus30 s

        In official matches a neg gives the other team 4 points; here it comes off your score instead. In mixed mode (below) bonuses are buzzed and scored like toss-ups, with the bonus time to buzz.

        Question modes

        Pick one under Questions in the settings.

        • Mixed: buzz on everything (the default): toss-ups and bonuses shuffled together, every question a buzz-in race. Team games always use this.
        • Toss-ups, then bonuses: get a toss-up right to earn its bonus, like a real round.
        • Toss-ups only or Bonuses only.
        • Visual bonuses only: just the bonuses with a picture.

        Round stages are picked under Rounds: RR (round robin, prelims, seeding) and early, mid and late DE, each tournament's elimination rounds split into thirds in order. Finals always count as late DE. For tournaments that only number their rounds (like MIT and Berkeley) it's an estimate: their first 5 rounds count as round robin.

        Visual bonuses

        Some bonuses come with a picture, like the handout in a real round: a diagram, graph, structure or photo. It shows as soon as the bonus starts (in listen mode too) and stays up after the answer. Click it to see it bigger; Esc closes it.

        • You get 30 seconds for a visual bonus.
        • They're on by default. Untick Visual bonuses in the settings to leave them out; a toss-up whose bonus was visual then gets another bonus in the same subject.
        • On the Questions page, pick Visual bonus under Type to see only these.

        Read aloud

        Turn on Read questions aloud for listen mode, like a real round: your browser's built-in voice reads the question, choices included, and nothing shows on screen until the answer is revealed. Then the whole question appears, with a mark where you buzzed and the unread part dimmed.

        • Buzz any time while it's read; the time to buzz starts when the voice stops.
        • Voice speed sets how fast it talks (1× is the voice's normal pace). Voices differ by device; ones installed on your device start faster than online ones, so the default picks them.
        • Words are said as written. Pronunciation guides printed in a question ("Telencephalon [tell-en-SEH-fuh-lawn]") are skipped, so some voices may mispronounce rare terms.

        In a room, the host turns it on and picks the voice and speed for everyone. Each device reads the question itself, with the host's voice if it has it, otherwise the closest one. Voices talk at different speeds, so a different voice is sped up or slowed down to keep the host's pace (it learns after a few sentences). A player who turns the voice off sees the question instead.

        On an iPhone, turn off silent mode to hear the voice.

        Questions

        Browsing the question bank

        The Questions page lists every question. Filter by subject, subcategory, type (including visual bonuses), set, round or difficulty, or search for words in a question or answer.

        • Sort by orders the results: packet order, easiest or hardest, newest or oldest set, set name, subject. The ⇅ button steps to the next order (Shift-click for the one before).
        • Pick a set and the Round filter lists its rounds (RR1, DE4, Finals 1…). Click a question's set and round ("MIT 2025, Round 3, #12") to see that whole round in order.
        • Every question has an ID, like 1qy21vz. Type one in the search box to find that question, for example to send it to a friend.
        • Page with the numbered buttons, Go to page or ← →; Per page sets how many show at once.

        Difficulty

        Every question has a difficulty number, shown in gold like 4.37. It says roughly how many teams at an average online invitational would get it right, with no other team buzzing and the usual time limits.

        every team

        4 teams in 10
        3
        1 in 4
        5
        1 in 10
        7
        1 in 25
        10

        A typical question is about 2.5, very few go past 8, and there's no top. A set's or round's number is the average of its questions.

        • In practice you see it after the question is read, with the set and round, so it can't affect when you buzz.
        • It's next to every question on the Questions and Saved pages, and next to sets and rounds in the filters and the Packets list in the settings.
        • On the Questions page, drag the two handles of the Difficulty slider to show a range (say 3 to 5, or 7 and up). Its columns show how many questions sit at each level.

        How it's rated. The number comes from the question itself, not from anyone's answers, so it's the same for everyone. Each question was rated by an AI reading it against example questions at every level of the scale. A rare answer you have to come up with yourself counts most; a routine calculation counts low however technical it looks, and a long question, a picture or a story setup doesn't make a question hard by itself. Questions added since the last rating get an estimate from their wording until they're rated. It's a good guide, not a measurement: a question can still be easy for you and rated hard.

        Saved questions

        Press the star next to a question, or B while practicing, to save it. The Saved page lists them, where you can review them or practice just those. They're kept in this browser.

        Reporting a problem

        Wrong answer key, garbled math, a missing picture, a bad ruling? Press Report on the question (after the answer, in This session, or on the Questions and Saved pages), pick what's wrong and add details if you like. Reports go straight to the site's fix list; the button then says Reported ✓.

        Playing together

        Rooms

        Rooms connect browsers directly, so there's no account or sign-up. Create a room and share its code; friends join with it on the Rooms page.

        • The person who creates the room runs it. If they close their tab, the room ends.
        • Turn on Teams (A vs B) to play team against team: every question is buzz-in, and a wrong answer locks out the whole team.
        • Your hosting settings are remembered on this device for your next room; your solo settings stay separate.

        Can't connect? School Wi-Fi, VPNs like Cloudflare WARP and managed Chromebooks block direct connections, so rooms switch to a relay server on their own. If joining still fails with "this device couldn't connect to the host", the network blocks the relay too: try a phone hotspot or a home network.

        Science Bowl and Ocean Science Bowl

        The SB / OSB switch at the top picks the competition. SB (the default) is the National Science Bowl bank. OSB is the National Ocean Sciences Bowl bank, with its own subjects (biology, chemistry, geology, physical oceanography, technology, social sciences and geography) and scoring: 4 points a toss-up, 6 a bonus, −4 for a neg.

        Each competition keeps its own settings and stats. Some OSB toss-ups are in quiz bowl style: long, starting hard and getting easier, so buzz as soon as you know it. In a room everyone plays the host's competition, so switch before creating or joining one.

        About

        Without internet

        Checking whether pts is saved for offline use…

        Once pts has loaded with internet, it keeps working without it: practice, every setting, both question banks, visual bonuses, saved questions and stats. Rooms, Report, Search Google and the online badge need a connection. Each time you open pts online it picks up new questions on its own.

        iPhone or iPad: open pts in Safari, tap Share → Add to Home Screen, then open it once from the new icon while you have internet so it can save everything (about 27 MB); the line above says when it's done. After that it works anywhere, even in airplane mode. The home-screen app keeps its own stats and saved questions, separate from Safari's.
