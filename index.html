        How it works

        Each question starts with its subject, type and format ("Physics, Toss-up, Short answer"), then the question is read one word at a time. You can turn the lead-in off in the settings. Buzz the moment you know the answer, then type it and press Enter. Answers are checked automatically; if the checker gets it wrong, use I was right or I was wrong to fix the ruling. Once the answer is shown, Search Google looks the question up in a new tab.

        Question modes

        By default, toss-ups and bonuses are shuffled together and every question is a buzz-in race: bonuses are buzzed and scored like toss-ups, but you get the bonus time (20 seconds) to buzz after a bonus is read instead of the toss-up's 5. Team games always use this mode. In the settings panel you can switch to toss-ups followed by a bonus for whoever gets them right, toss-ups only, or bonuses only.

        Read aloud

        Turn on Read questions aloud in the settings for listen mode, like a real round: the question is only read aloud by your browser's built-in voice, multiple-choice options included, and nothing appears on screen until the answer is revealed. Then the full question shows, with a mark where you buzzed and the part not yet read dimmed. Buzz any time while it's being read; the time to buzz starts when the voice finishes. Voice speed sets how fast it talks (1× is the voice's normal pace). Voices differ by device; ones installed on your device start faster than online ones, so the default prefers them. In a room the host turns it on and picks the voice and speed for everyone, and the host's voice paces the round. Each device reads the question itself, with the host's voice if it has it (voices come with the device and browser), otherwise the closest one it has, and says so; and a player who turns the voice off on their device sees the question instead. On an iPhone, turn off silent mode to hear it.

        Scoring

        Scoring follows the National Science Bowl defaults, and you can change each value in the settings panel.

        • Correct toss-up: +4. In mixed mode bonuses score the same way; in toss-up-then-bonus mode a correct toss-up also earns you the bonus.
        • Wrong answer after buzzing in before the question finished (a neg): −4. In official matches the other team gets those 4 points instead; here it comes off your score. Wrong answers after the question finished cost nothing.
        • Correct bonus, when bonuses are played as bonuses: +10.
        • You have 5 seconds to buzz after a toss-up finishes, and 20 seconds to answer a bonus.

        Answering multiple choice

        Type the letter (W, X, Y or Z) or the option's text. Either counts, as in real matches.

        Answer checking

        Multiple choice is strict: type the letter (W, X, Y or Z) or the option exactly as written. Capitalization, spacing and punctuation don't matter, and a number option can be typed as any equal number in simple form (0.5 for 1/2), but not as an unsimplified fraction like 2/4 or an expression.

        Short answers lean toward accepting. The checker allows typos and plurals, lead-ins like "it's" or "the answer is", optional words in parentheses, the answer's listed alternates, and equivalent or more precise numbers (0.5, 1/2 and 5 x 10^-1 all match; 9.81 counts for 9.8). For "select all that apply" questions, type the numbers any way you like (Roman numerals too, so 13 works for "I and III"): 1 and 3, 1, 3, 31 or 13 all work, and when the answer is "all", typing every number (123) counts too. You can also answer with the statements' names (alpha, beta, gamma for 1, 2, 3). For "order" or "rank" questions the order matters. Math answers are compared by value: sqrt(3)/2, √3/2 or 0.866 all match an answer of √3⁄2 (decimals need at least 3 significant figures). Answers with variables are compared the same way: 1/4 omega, omega/4, w/4 and 0.25ω all match ω/4, and 1/2 mv^2 matches ½mv² (but M/m doesn't match m/M). The thing the question asks for can be left off or added: for "What soil horizon..." both O and O horizon work, and Doppler counts for "Doppler effect" when the question says "Name this effect". Very short answers like element symbols still have to be exact, and a near miss that is really a different term (troposphere for stratosphere, mitosis for meiosis) isn't forgiven as a typo. If a ruling is wrong, use I was right.

        Rounds

        Pick round stages in the settings panel: RR (round robin, prelims, seeding), and early, mid and late DE, which split each tournament's elimination rounds into thirds in order. Finals and championship rounds always count as late DE. Tournaments that only number their rounds (like MIT and Berkeley) are an estimate: their first 5 rounds count as round robin.

        Saved questions

        Press the star next to a question, or B while practicing, to save it. Saved questions are listed under Saved, where you can review them or practice just those. They're stored in this browser.

        Rooms

        Rooms connect browsers directly to each other, so there's no account or server to set up. The person who creates the room runs it: if they close their tab, the room ends. Turn on Teams in the room settings to play A versus B: every question is buzz-in, and a wrong answer locks out the whole team. The settings you use when hosting are remembered on this device, so the next room you create starts with them; your solo practice settings stay separate.

        Science Bowl and Ocean Science Bowl

        The SB / OSB switch at the top picks the competition. SB (the default) is the National Science Bowl bank; OSB is the National Ocean Sciences Bowl bank, with its subjects (biology, chemistry, geology, physical oceanography, technology, social sciences and geography) and its scoring: 4 points a toss-up, 6 a bonus, −4 for a neg, 5 seconds to buzz and 20 to answer a bonus. Each competition keeps its own settings, room settings and stats. Some OSB questions are in quiz bowl style: long toss-ups that start hard and get easier, so buzz as soon as you know it. Switch before creating or joining a room; everyone in a room plays the host's competition.

        Browsing the question bank

        On the Questions page, filter by subject, type, set or round, or search for words in a question or answer. Page through the results with the numbered buttons, jump straight to a page with Go to page, or use the ← and → keys. Per page sets how many questions each page shows.

        The Round filter offers round stages (RR, early, mid and late DE). Pick a set first and it also lists every round that set has, such as RR1, DE4 or Finals 1.
