          How it works

          Questions are read one word at a time. Buzz the moment you know the answer, then type it and press Enter. Answers are checked automatically; if the checker gets it wrong, use I was right or I was wrong to fix the ruling.

          Question modes

          By default, toss-ups and bonuses are shuffled together and every question is a buzz-in race: bonuses are buzzed and scored like toss-ups, but you get the bonus time (20 seconds) to buzz after a bonus is read instead of the toss-up's 5. Team games always use this mode. In the settings panel you can switch to toss-ups followed by a bonus for whoever gets them right, toss-ups only, or bonuses only.

          Read aloud

          Turn on Read questions aloud in the settings to hear each question in your browser's built-in voice while the words appear. The voice follows the reading speed, stops when someone buzzes, and picks up where it left off if the question continues. Voices differ by device; pick one under the checkbox. In a room the host turns it on for everyone, and each player can mute it on their own device.

          Scoring

          Scoring follows the National Science Bowl defaults, and you can change each value in the settings panel.

          • Correct toss-up: +4. In mixed mode bonuses score the same way; in toss-up-then-bonus mode a correct toss-up also earns you the bonus.
          • Wrong answer after buzzing in before the question finished (an interrupt): −4. In official matches the other team gets those 4 points instead; here it comes off your score. Wrong answers after the question finished cost nothing.
          • Correct bonus, when bonuses are played as bonuses: +10.
          • You have 5 seconds to buzz after a toss-up finishes, and 20 seconds to answer a bonus.

          Answering multiple choice

          Type the letter (W, X, Y or Z) or the option's text. Either counts, as in real matches.

          Answer checking

          The checker accepts small typos on longer answers, optional words shown in parentheses, the answer's listed alternates, and equivalent numbers (0.5, 1/2 and 5 x 10^-1 all match). Short answers like element symbols must be exact. For "which of the following" lists, enter the numbers in any order, like 1, 3.

          Rooms

          Rooms connect browsers directly to each other, so there's no account or server to set up. The person who creates the room runs it: if they close their tab, the room ends. Turn on Teams in the room settings to play A versus B: every question is buzz-in, and a wrong answer locks out the whole team.

          Where the questions come from

          Questions load from a Google Sheet the site owner maintains. Packets are added with an AI importer that reads each PDF and fills in the sheet.
